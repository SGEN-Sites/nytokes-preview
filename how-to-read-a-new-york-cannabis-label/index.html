How to Read a New York Cannabis Label

Chapter 01

Why the label matters

Every product sold at a licensed New York dispensary carries a label, and it tells you more than most people realize. What’s in it, how much, when it was tested, and how to use it. Once you know where to look, it takes about ten seconds.

Here’s how to read one, section by section. If anything’s unclear, bring the package to the counter at either store and a budtender will walk you through it.

Chapter 02

1. Product type and amount

Start with the basics: what the product is (flower, pre-roll, vape, edible, drink, concentrate) and how much is in the package. Flower and pre-rolls are listed by weight. Vapes and concentrates by weight or volume. Drinks by volume.

Chapter 03

2. Cannabinoid content

This is where you’ll find THC and, if present, CBD. For flower and vapes it’s usually shown as a percentage. Remember that the THC number is only part of the picture. Terpenes, format and your own experience all shape how a product feels. We go deeper on that in what THC percentage actually means.

Chapter 04

3. Servings (edibles and drinks)

Edibles and drinks list milligrams per serving and per package. Those are two very different numbers, so read both. A package can contain several servings, and the per-serving amount is the one to pay attention to. Start with one serving and give it time before you have more. Effects vary from person to person.

Chapter 05

4. Lot number and test date

Each product has a lot or batch number, along with testing information. That ties the package you’re holding to a specific batch and the lab results for it. Legal New York products are tested by licensed labs before they reach the shelf. Our post on what lab-tested cannabis actually means explains what that testing covers.

Chapter 06

5. The QR code

Many packages have a QR code. Scan it and you’ll usually land on the lab results, often called a certificate of analysis or COA, for that batch. It’s the most detailed view of what’s in the product.

Chapter 07

6. Warnings and the universal symbol

New York requires warning statements on cannabis packaging, plus a universal symbol that marks the product as containing THC. Read the warnings. They cover things like keeping the product away from kids and pets and not driving after use.

Chapter 08

7. Who made it

The label also names the brand and the licensed company that produced or packaged the product. If you like something, that’s how you find it again.

Chapter 09

Quick checklist

  • What is it, and how much is in the package?
  • What’s the THC (and CBD) content?
  • For edibles and drinks: how many mg per serving, and how many servings?
  • What’s the lot number and test date?
  • Is there a QR code for the lab results?
  • What do the warnings say?
Chapter 10

Bring it to the counter

Still not sure? Bring any package, or a photo of one, to either store and ask.
